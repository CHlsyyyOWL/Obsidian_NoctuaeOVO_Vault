## History of MW
USA
- introducing minimum wage as regulation from more developed northern state of America to southern state 
- making social well-being and safety more transparent

Latin America
- introducing more of political economica issues
- Political economy considerations
	- State intervention in labour relations was also motivated by an effort to make workers look at the state instead of unions to protect their interests
	- Unions were often linked to radical political ideologies questioning state’s authority

Europe, Africa, Asia
- Europe introduced after WWII
- Africa followed regulation linked to colonial powers
- Asian adopted slowly until recent decades

## Debate of MW
- Long-standing tradition arguing that a minimum wage will reduce employment if it is set above the competitive level
	- Mill 1848
	-  Marshall, 1897
	- Stigler, 1946

### - Neoclassical - Hicks 1932
- Key assumption (or so-called “price theory”) 
	- Low-skilled workers are homogenous 
	- Many firms compete for the same type of workers 
	- Low-skilled jobs are standardized

- George Stigler 1946 - Price theory
	- No free lunch
	- Monimum wgae decreases enployment and leads to efficiency loss

- Richard Lester 1947
	- Instead of analysing models, look at the data 
	- Ask managers about what determines employment 
	- Most important factor: the output demand 
	- Less than 20% reported the labour cost as the most important 
	- **(small) increase of labour cost will have a limited employment effect**

- Milton Friedman (1953): “Essays in Positive Economics” 
	- Assumptions can be unrealistic and simplified 
		- The billiard player analogy
