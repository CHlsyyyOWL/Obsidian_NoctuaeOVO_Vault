## Part 1. Research question

Did the Social Credit System pilots narrow the gap in bank borrowing between politically connected and unconnected private listed firms in China?

## Part 2. The broader literature

My question sits within the literature on state capacity and state–business relations. Economists and political scientists treat state capacity as a condition for development, and much of this capacity rests on how much the state knows about the actors it governs (Besley & Persson, 2009; Lee & Zhang, 2017). For firms, Evans (1995) argues that a developmental state gathers such information through close ties with business, but the same ties expose it to capture. Empirical work therefore asks whether these ties allocate resources efficiently or extract rents, and finds that connected firms obtain more bank credit, often from government-owned banks (Khwaja & Mian, 2005). China is a central case, since local officials have granted special deals to connected firms (Bai et al., 2020) and connections help private firms obtain loans where institutions are weak (Li et al., 2008). The Social Credit System gives the state another channel, because it publishes standardised compliance records for every firm. Pilot studies find that unconnected firms gain more trade credit after the reform (Wang et al., 2026), while credit scores themselves reward political connections (Lin & Milhaupt, 2023). My project tests which pattern holds for bank credit, and therefore whether impersonal information can replace personal ties.

## Part 3. Summaries of five key papers

### Paper 1

**Title and reference.** Do lenders favor politically connected firms? Rent provision in an emerging financial market. Khwaja, A. I., & Mian, A. (2005). *The Quarterly Journal of Economics*, 120(4), 1371–1411. https://doi.org/10.1162/003355305775097524

**Research question.** Do banks lend more to politically connected firms, and does this lending reflect better information about these firms or the provision of rents?

**Contribution.** Earlier work showed that political connections raise firm value (Faccio, 2006), but it could not observe the channel through which firms gain. This paper observes individual loans, so it can test whether connected firms receive more credit and whether that credit performs worse, which separates rent provision from better information.

**Methodology.** Econometric. The authors regress borrowing and default on a firm's political status, and compare the same firm across government and private banks and over time. They also use variation in the politician's electoral strength and whether his party holds power.

**Data.** Loan-level data covering almost all corporate lending in Pakistan, more than 90,000 firms, 1996 to 2002. A firm is political if one of its directors ran in an election.

**Main findings.** Political firms borrow 45% more and default 50% more often. This advantage appears only at government banks, and it grows with the politician's strength and when his party is in power. The authors reject a social lending explanation and estimate the cost at 0.3% to 1.9% of GDP each year.

**Relevance to your project.** This paper shows that a connection advantage in bank credit can be a pure rent, in which case public credit records would not remove it. Since state-owned banks dominate lending in China, my design needs to separate this possibility from information substitution.

### Paper 2

**Title and reference.** Political connections, financing and firm performance: Evidence from Chinese private firms. Li, H., Meng, L., Wang, Q., & Zhou, L.-A. (2008). *Journal of Development Economics*, 87(2), 283–299. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jdeveco.2007.03.001

**Research question.** Does Communist Party membership of private entrepreneurs improve firm performance in China, and through which channels does it work?

**Contribution.** The paper extends the connection literature to Chinese private firms, and it links the value of connections to the quality of local market and legal institutions, which suggests that connections partly substitute for weak institutions.

**Methodology.** Econometric. The authors regress firm performance on the entrepreneur's Party membership, controlling for human capital and other firm and owner characteristics. They then test whether membership helps firms obtain loans and raises confidence in the legal system, and whether its effect differs across regions with different institutional quality.

**Data.** A nationwide survey of Chinese private firms [to verify, survey year and sample size].

**Main findings.** Party membership has a positive effect on firm performance after controlling for human capital. Members find it easier to obtain loans from banks and other state institutions, and they have more confidence in the legal system. The effect of membership on performance is larger in regions with weaker market institutions and weaker legal protection.

**Relevance to your project.** This paper is the starting point of my research idea, because it suggests that banks treat connections as assurance where they lack other information. If a public credit record provides that assurance for every firm, the gap between connected and unconnected firms should narrow. My connection measure follows studies of listed firms rather than Party membership.

### Paper 3

**Title and reference.** Information sharing and credit: Firm-level evidence from transition countries. Brown, M., Jappelli, T., & Pagano, M. (2009). *Journal of Financial Intermediation*, 18(2), 151–172. https://econpapers.repec.org/article/eeejfinin/v_3a18_3ay_3a2009_3ai_3a2_3ap_3a151-172.htm

**Research question.** Does information sharing among lenders improve firms' access to credit and lower its cost in transition economies?

**Contribution.** Earlier evidence on information sharing came from cross-country data on aggregate lending (Jappelli & Pagano, 2002). This paper uses firm-level data, so it can test the theoretical prediction that information sharing matters most for firms that banks find hard to assess.

**Methodology.** Econometric. The authors relate firms' reported access to and cost of credit to a country index of information sharing, and interact the index with firm transparency, using instruments for transparency. A panel with firm fixed effects then relates changes in credit constraints between 2002 and 2005 to changes in information sharing.

**Data.** EBRD and World Bank Business Environment and Enterprise Performance Survey, 5,717 firms in 24 transition countries in 2002, with a panel of 1,333 firms surveyed again in 2005. The information sharing index comes from the World Bank Doing Business database, 1996 to 2004.

**Main findings.** Information sharing is associated with easier access to credit and lower credit costs. Moving the index from 0 to 4.6 raises the access measure by about 30% of its mean, and the full range raises debt to assets by 4.2 percentage points against a mean of 9.3%. The effect is stronger for opaque firms and in countries with weak legal systems.

**Relevance to your project.** This paper provides the mechanism behind my first hypothesis, because public credit information helps most where banks cannot otherwise assess a firm. If unconnected firms are the ones banks find hard to assess, the Social Credit System should benefit them more.

### Paper 4

**Title and reference.** Social credit and trade credit: A coevolutionary perspective. Wang, Q. S., Chen, L., Lai, S., & Anderson, H. D. (2026). *Journal of Business Ethics*, 204(2), 273–308. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10551-025-05968-0

**Research question.** How do firms' trade credit decisions respond to China's social credit reform, which rewards or punishes firms based on external monitoring of their credit behaviour?

**Contribution.** The paper treats the Social Credit System as a credit information reform and asks whether it changes how suppliers, rather than regulators, judge firms. It also identifies the channels through which the reform changes suppliers' decisions.

**Methodology.** Econometric. The paper uses the designation of pilot cities as a quasi-natural experiment and compares firms in pilot and non-pilot cities before and after the reform in a difference-in-differences design [to verify, exact specification]. It then tests channels and compares effects across types of firms.

**Data.** Chinese firms in pilot and non-pilot cities [to verify, data source, sample and period].

**Main findings.** Firms in pilot cities gain more access to trade credit after the reform. The authors trace this to lower default risk, better information quality and stronger legal protection. The increase is larger for young, small, private and politically unconnected firms, and the extra trade credit brings strategic and performance benefits.

**Relevance to your project.** This paper suggests that the Social Credit System helps lenders assess firms that lack political connections. However, it studies trade credit and treats connections only as one dimension of heterogeneity, so my project tests whether the same pattern holds for bank loans and makes connections the main comparison.

### Paper 5

**Title and reference.** China's corporate social credit system: The dawn of surveillance state capitalism? Lin, L. Y.-H., & Milhaupt, C. J. (2023). *The China Quarterly*, 256, 835–853. https://doi.org/10.1017/S030574102300067X

**Research question.** What does China's corporate social credit system reward in practice, and do politically connected firms receive higher credit scores?

**Contribution.** Instead of relying on policy documents, this paper studies the scores that local governments actually publish, and it argues that the system may work as a tool of state control over firms rather than only as a compliance record.

**Methodology.** Econometric with document analysis. The authors regress published credit scores and their sub-scores on political connections, state ownership and governance features, and they examine the scoring rules behind each category.

**Data.** Published credit scores of 531 listed firms in Zhejiang as of 1 July 2021, with a regression sample of 414 firms.

**Main findings.** Politically connected firms score about 10.5 points higher, and the whole gap comes from a social responsibility category that rewards government awards and donations to Party-sanctioned causes. Connected firms do not have better compliance records, and state ownership is not significant in the full model. The authors describe their results as preliminary because they cover one province.

**Relevance to your project.** This paper provides my rival hypothesis, since it shows that credit scores can carry political alignment rather than compliance alone. If so, the Social Credit System may preserve or widen the advantage of connected firms, which a single-province cross-section without loan data cannot test.
