# Homework 3 Brief
Due Seminar 3 (16 Nov). Around 1,200 words, excluding bibliography.

**What this is**
For this homework you'll write a short literature review of around 1,200 words (excluding bibliography), built around your own dissertation research question. Think of it as a dress rehearsal for the literature section of your Research Proposal in January.

A literature review is not a list of papers you've read. It's an argument about where a body of research stands: what it does well, where it falls short, and why your question is worth asking.

**What your review needs to do**
1. Introduction
Set out your topic and state your research question. Then give the reader a reason to care, using stylised facts, a case study, or the policy relevance of your question.
Next, explain how you approached the review. What is its scope? Which papers did you include, which did you leave out, and why? Then explain how you have organised the literature. You might group papers by method, findings, proposed mechanisms, chronology, or discipline. Choose whichever grouping best brings out the debate.

2. Main body
Now review the literature using the categories you set out. A summary table or other visualisation can work well here. End the section by stepping back. Where does the literature stand overall, and what are the gaps?

3. Conclusion
Pull it together. Briefly restate where the literature stands, and explain how your own research fits in: which gap it addresses, or which debate it contributes to.
